What Happens at Your Exam

The appointment itself is more routine than most people expect. We start by checking your photo identification and going through whatever immunization records you've brought. Dr. Weiss then performs the physical examination USCIS requires — a general assessment covering your eyes, ears, heart, lungs, abdomen, skin and neurological function. Blood is drawn and a urine sample collected in our own office for the required laboratory testing, and tuberculosis screening is completed at the same visit. Finally we go through which vaccinations USCIS requires at your age against what your records already show. Most Wayne patients are in and out in about half an hour.

Documents to Bring to Your Appointment

Three things matter most: valid government photo identification (an unexpired passport is best, a driver's license works), any immunization records you hold — foreign-language records are perfectly fine — and documentation of any significant medical history, particularly a previous positive TB test or treatment for a chronic condition. Add a written list of medications you currently take. If you have already printed Form I-693, leave it unsigned; the signature must be made in front of the civil surgeon. Relatives and interpreters are welcome to accompany you.

Timing Your Exam Correctly

Getting the timing right saves money. USCIS stopped applying a calendar expiry to forms signed on or after November 1, 2023, but policy effective June 11, 2025 ties each completed I-693 to the single application it was filed with — it will not carry over to a future petition, and a denial or withdrawal means a new exam. Anything signed before November 1, 2023 remains under the old two-year rule. So the sensible pattern for Wayne applicants is to book the exam once the rest of the application package is ready, or when USCIS asks for it, rather than months ahead.
