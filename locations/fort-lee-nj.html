How Long Your Sealed I-693 Stays Valid

This is the question we are asked most, and the answer is not what most websites still say. A Form I-693 signed on or after November 1, 2023 does not expire on a calendar date the way older forms did. Since June 11, 2025, however, USCIS treats the form as valid only for the application it accompanied — it cannot be reused for a different or future filing, and a denied or withdrawn case means starting the medical exam over. Forms signed before November 1, 2023 still follow the previous two-year rule. If you are applying from Fort Lee, time your exam to your filing rather than getting ahead of it.

Accuracy Is the Whole Point

Fort Lee's Korean, Chinese and South Asian communities file continuously, often with attorney representation. The paperwork is where immigration medical exams go wrong. A physician who is not USCIS-designated invalidates the exam outright. Beyond that, the recurring failures are mundane and entirely avoidable: an edition of the form USCIS no longer accepts, an unsigned or inconsistently dated document, an incomplete TB classification, a vaccination section left blank, a missing age-required test, a positive tuberculosis screen filed without its chest x-ray, or a broken seal on the envelope. Every one of them produces a Request for Evidence and pushes a case back by months — and since June 2025, because the form is valid only for the application it accompanied, a failed filing means a completely new exam rather than a reusable one. Fort Lee applicants are not buying thirty minutes of a doctor's time; they are buying a document that clears on the first submission.

This is precisely what immigration attorneys care about. When a lawyer has spent months preparing a case, the last thing they want is a Request for Evidence caused by an avoidable gap in the paperwork. What they want is a form that comes back complete, internally consistent and on the current edition — so we check the document against USCIS requirements before the envelope is sealed.
